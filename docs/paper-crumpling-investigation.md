# Paper crumpling: architecture investigation

**Decision: approved, with physical crease-adaptive remeshing.** The evidence and estimates below describe the pre-implementation investigation; the revised architecture refines the simulation, not just the display skin.

**Research and licensing.** Narain, Samii & O'Brien, [Adaptive Anisotropic Remeshing for Cloth Simulation](http://graphics.eecs.berkeley.edu/resources/ARCSim/), and Narain, Pfaff & O'Brien, [Folding and Crumpling Adaptive Sheets](http://graphics.cs.berkeley.edu/papers/Narain-FCA-2013-07/Narain-FCA-2013-07.pdf), identify bending lock, diffusion of plastic creases during remeshing, and jitter when reprojecting a stiff sheet. ARCSim uses a plastic embedding distinct from both material coordinates and current pose, a curvature/strain sizing metric, and contact processing. The important constraints here are to keep the printed material chart fixed, transport signed bend history, preserve contacted features and test swept motion rather than just end-of-step overlap. The [Berkeley distribution](http://graphics.eecs.berkeley.edu/resources/ARCSim/) is offered for non-profit use with licensing and attribution requirements. Hyprimate does not import its code, datasets, or SELF-CCD library; its bounded interactive solver is independently implemented.

## Baseline before replacement (957c488)

The removed `paper_takes.bin` asset was `PCRT` version 1: 57 vertices per side, 48 poses, six takes (5,614,316 bytes). The old `PaperCrumple` chose the nearest aspect family, selected and mirrored a take, sampled four poses, and projected it. Opening followed the same path in reverse. The final part also shrank and faded. Increasing the pose count would not have given the surface a strain state or let the layers push on each other.

The old shared projector sorted 6,272 triangles by mean depth and set a crease threshold on neighboring face normals. It expanded the indexed sheet into two XY/UV streams, 18,816 corners each, then drew the window and lighting into separate color targets. Mean triangle depth could not represent a change in frontmost surface within a triangle. Simply subdividing those faces linearly would add cost without rounding the silhouette or creating better folds.

- **Measured here, current existing build:** standalone caller linked against `build/libhyprimate-core.a` (GCC, RelWithDebInfo, `-O2`). One 1280×720 window, default close, fixed 1/120 s input, 59 active frames per event, 32 consecutive seeds after two warmup events, reused buffers. Individual frame elapsed times from `steady_clock`, no pacing. For `advance + frame`, p50/p95/p99 were **0.977/1.065/1.201 ms**. Including `fill_sheet_mesh` for both streams: **1.078/1.170/1.366 ms**. Not a live plugin benchmark: no bounds scan, GL calls, capture, presentation, cold-start timing, or other windows.
- **Recorded previously:** `build/dev/bench/first.json`, 20 runs: median ~36.12 ms for 40 scripted Paper updates/projections. Its ~0.90 ms is a run average, not a frame percentile. The same file has ~652 ms for a **complete** Jolt Cloth open recording including setup and waiting, not for one tick. Neither benchmark measures custom paper mechanics.
- **Hardware observed:** Ryzen 5 5600X (6 cores/12 threads). The running Hyprland 0.57.0 renderer identifies itself as RTX 2070 SUPER, OpenGL ES 3.2, NVIDIA 615.78.08, with `GL_EXT_disjoint_timer_query`. The installed 5060 Ti and 5070 Ti are not the compositor renderer. Active modes: 1920×1080 at 100 Hz and two 1600×900 at 74.89 Hz, all scale 1. The live plugin inode is not the current build artifact; no live-session benchmark or reload was performed.
- **GPU reference, not a Paper measurement:** [prior GPU log](../.project-manager/evidence/planet-hop/final-gpu.log) and [project record](../.project-manager/progress.md#L720-L726) report 0.508 ms at 1440p and 1.064 ms at 4K for the high-quality cached Planet Hop scene on the 2070 SUPER. Different shaders, geometry and overdraw; excludes native capture. Neither video timestamps nor whole-compositor GPU utilization isolate Paper cost.

## Alternatives

**A — Jolt-driven, CPU.** [Pinned Jolt 5.6.0](../CMakeLists.txt#L20-L52) already ships for Cloth. Its [sheet constraints](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/v5.6.0/Jolt/Physics/SoftBody/SoftBodySharedSettings.h#L40-L71) provide stretch, shear and dihedral bending. Initial hinge angles are part of shared settings, intended to stay constant; an angle is not a yield law or a per-window crease history. Its [contact collector](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/v5.6.0/Jolt/Physics/SoftBody/SoftBodyMotionProperties.cpp#L120-L265) accepts rigid bodies, not another fold of the sheet. Rigid guides could push it inward, but cannot keep paper layers apart. `CustomUpdate` can run on the caller thread but still uses a physics system; a full world update has a job barrier. Neither belongs on the compositor thread unbounded. A separate CPU job must publish the latest pose and GL must still upload and draw it. No new package is necessary, but Jolt's global lifetime currently belongs to Cloth: Paper must not create a second factory or import Cloth internals. Medium confidence in an elastic sheet; low in the specified tight wad without writing contact and plasticity outside Jolt. That would leave two cooperating solvers to debug. Do not copy Cloth's full-motion recording design.

**B — analytic GLES vertex deformation.** Generate per-event compression fronts and curved hinges in material coordinates. Evaluate local rotations about those hinges in the vertex shader; use a stable force field and continuous phase functions rather than drawing new noise each frame. Rigid rotations around an isolated straight fold can preserve length. Several finite folds with intersecting supports generally cannot be composed this way without distorting the intervening material. A depth buffer makes the nearest fragment win but provides no reaction force; a corner can pass through the sheet and change sides. Local springs and separate release schedules improve timing, not contact. High confidence in implementation and clean endpoints; low in plausible dense crumpling and reversal. Easy to maintain until intersection and hysteresis exceptions accumulate. A genuinely stateful GPU constraint solver is a different alternative: compute/SSBOs or ping-pong textures, repeated dependent dispatches/passes, barriers, a contact broadphase and a CPU bounds/replay strategy. Possible on this GPU; a much larger compatibility and debugging commitment than the existing ES 3.0 shader path. No reason to require it for hundreds of control points without profiling first.

**C — specialized CPU sheet + GPU surface.** Keep the contact solve small and independent of both screen resolution and Hyprland. Spend GPU geometry where it improves projected curvature and the silhouette. CPU owns particle positions/velocities, strain, signed hinge memory and persistent contacts; GPU owns refinement, visibility and lighting. Both are necessary: the GPU must not invent the macroscopic fold that the CPU is supposedly simulating. Medium-high confidence in integration and testability, medium in achieving the visual target at the tightest sizes. Dense contact, directional bias in hinge topology and cold opening are the main risks. Unlike A and B, the proposed paper-specific work addresses those risks directly.

Physical references: [XPBD](https://mmacklin.com/xpbd.pdf) motivates control of compliance independently of display rate; it does not automatically make a sheet paper or guarantee convergence in a fixed budget. [Narain, Pfaff & O'Brien](https://tobiaspfaff.com/publication/narain-2013-folding/) motivate resolving emerging creases. [Schreck et al.](https://inria.hal.science/hal-01202571/document) explicitly distinguish length preservation, sharp features and plastic memory. Their hybrid prototype reported **1–10 fps**, no collision handling on the reconstructed surface, and some visible changes from coarse remeshing. These are design references, not performance predictions for Hyprimate.

## Cost model — estimates only

Warm, one 1280×720 drawable at scale 1 on the hardware above, roughly 1×–3× local overdraw, 8k–24k indexed render triangles, one 256² light-depth pass when shaded. For A/C, roughly 250–500 simulation vertices, nearly 3 stretch edges and 3 interior bend candidates per vertex, 3–5 passes per 120 Hz tick (roughly 5k–15k constraint visits before contacts). Compare A using a coarse body **plus external contact/crease work**, not Cloth's full 57² solve. B assumes ~8–20 active force/fold descriptors, no iterative contact. All three use the same depth-capable drawing contract. Timings are rounded engineering estimates based on these workload sizes, local CPU measurements and the GPU reference, not percentiles, guarantees or a benchmark of any proposal.

| Architecture | Worker CPU per 1/120 s tick, usual / stress | Per-window GPU per displayed frame, usual / stress | Approx. per-window CPU working set, excluding shared runtime |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A: Jolt + missing mechanics | 0.4–1.5 / 1.5–4+ ms | 0.12–0.55 / 0.55–1.5 ms | 0.25–1+ MiB for coarse body, constraints, contacts and poses; world/temp allocator extra |
| B: analytic GPU | No solve; <0.03 ms CPU to update phase/forces per frame | 0.15–0.65 / 0.65–1.8 ms | <8 KiB for descriptors; render topology shareable |
| C: custom hybrid | 0.25–0.9 / 0.9–2.5 ms | 0.12–0.55 / 0.55–1.5 ms | 0.12–0.5 MiB for dynamic state, topology, capped contacts and published poses |

Allow another **0.03–0.15 ms of render-thread submission per window**, up to roughly 0.45 ms in a stressed warm case, for any path. Startup allocation, driver stalls, shader compilation, contention from other apps, retained capture, compositor composition and vsync are excluded. Time ranges overlap deliberately. Jolt might beat a custom elastic solve; elasticity is not the disputed cost. Actual dense contact candidate counts, shader occupancy, first-map time and driver contention dominate uncertainty. Stress ranges are not hard upper bounds.

- **GPU work/memory:** one indexed, two-sided color/depth draw; one more geometry draw for the light-depth map when enabled. Mapped windows return that layer to the transformer; owned closes also composite a quad. Depth testing replaces mean-Z sort and the separate shade-layer tint. No readback or GPU→CPU bounds calculation. For A/C, stream approximately 8–32 KiB of controls per published pose using rotating storage; B sends only force descriptors. Allow roughly 0.15–0.8 MiB GPU for render parameter vertices/indices per resident mesh/tier, plus A/C control storage. At RGBA8 + four bytes/pixel for depth storage, one color/depth pair is **7.03 MiB at 720p, 15.82 MiB at 1080p, 63.28 MiB at 4K**. Color is a compositor work buffer, not necessarily a new allocation; private depth accounts for half. One 256² shadow target at four bytes/pixel is 0.25 MiB. These exclude source captures, driver padding and higher-precision color. Bound depth targets by pixel budget; reuse between nonoverlapping draws, keep nested capture scratch separate, don't retain one maximum-size pair per window. Across windows the color buffers may have overlapping lifetimes.
- **Scaling:** A/C cost grows with constraints × passes plus contact candidates, not pixel count. Dense folds can make an unbounded neighbor search quadratic. Cap the broadphase enumeration **and** narrowphase work, prioritizing already-touching layers. B vertex cost grows with render vertices × active folds. For all three, fragment cost tracks covered pixels, samples and overdraw. At 4K there are nine times as many pixels as at 720p, not nine times as many control particles. Lower-power GPUs have not been characterized.
- **Refresh:** 60/120/144 Hz give **16.67/8.33/6.94 ms for the entire frame**. At 120 simulation ticks/sec A/C need two ticks per 60 Hz frame, one per 120 Hz frame, and five per six 144 Hz frames on average; interpolate every displayed frame. Four concurrent 720p windows at 120 Hz imply A **1.6–6.0 ms** or C **1.0–3.6 ms** aggregate worker work per tick, versus **0.48–2.2 ms** aggregate usual GPU work per displayed frame for either, before host work. For B the equivalent is **<0.12 ms** CPU force update and **0.6–2.6 ms** GPU. At 60 Hz double the tick work per displayed frame; at 144 Hz don't stretch a tick or reduce the simulation rate. CPU worker work and GPU time are neither necessarily serialized nor free: queue latency and snapshot age matter. Four stressed C windows could require **10 ms of worker work per 8.33 ms tick**; a per-window budget alone is insufficient. No promise of 144 Hz under arbitrary concurrent load.

## Recommended design: C

1. **Close under loads, not a scale transform.** Start in exact capture-space geometry with rest UVs and a shape-regular, irregularly triangulated control sheet fitted to its true aspect. Hold in-plane lengths strongly; let hinges bend elastically up to a threshold, then accumulate a signed change in their preferred angle. Seed smooth variation in strength and fiber direction. Several distributed grip regions press or gather at different times, including edge lifts and small out-of-plane loads to trigger buckling. A compliant shrinking envelope limits the extent but does not move every vertex toward a prescribed ball. Resistance, adjacent folds and contact determine which region yields. The final mass must retain area in overlapping layers. Cap attainable packing based on thickness and resolution, not a last-minute transform. Default spin should be restrained; any wobble reflects distributed torque and inertia.
2. **Contact is a simulation requirement.** Use a spatial hash of coarse, swept surface bounds, exclude topologically adjacent pieces, retain contact sides and apply separation with small effective thickness and friction. Include vertex–triangle and edge–edge cases; a vertex-only sphere test misses crossed edges. Limit imposed motion per substep. Bound per-cell visits and total contacts deterministically; when stressed, ease the *applied compression* before breaking established contacts. A depth buffer cannot repair inverted layer order. The coarse solver cannot promise exact continuous collision for every point on the reconstructed skin.
3. **Open by releasing a packed sheet.** A fresh map needs a compact first visible frame, not a flat-frame flash or an entire close simulated synchronously in `open()`. Construct a per-event compact initial condition: bounded, asymmetric, near-isometric packet folds in material coordinates, with layer order, separated thickness and stored signed bends. Fit to aspect, rotate fold axes and vary packet widths from the seed, then perform bounded contact relaxation. This produces **a starting condition, not a sequence to play**. First-frame initialization, the strain it introduces and whether it looks like a wad rather than an accordion are explicit validation gates. Release contacts/grips at different rates; edge guidance pulls out toward the true rectangle as hinge memory decays. Stronger late flat-rest constraints and damping deliver exact geometry and lighting continuously before native handoff. Do not dissolve the window to hide endpoint error. On close-during-open, retain displayed positions, velocity, rest UVs, hinge history and contact sides; blend in loads from the new close seed, don't regenerate the sheet.
4. **Render geometry, not triangle lighting.** Choose bounded refinement from size/aspect and projected curvature, stable during the event. Reconstruct a curved patch between solved edges, with position-continuous seams, separate tangents/normals across yielded ridges, and smooth normals within uncreased regions. Limit reconstruction displacement against contact clearance so the smooth skin does not breach another layer. This is not linear barycentric subdivision or a normal map over a polygonal outline. Use fixed material UVs, perspective-correct interpolation, front/back diffuse lighting, contact darkening and a small light-space depth map for cast fold shadows. Preserve native resolution and premultiplied alpha: transparent capture margins must not occlude paper; a translucent client should not become a pile of translucent tissue. Restore all GL state, including depth mask/function/clear value, and never attach depth to the compositor's own FBO. Full dynamic remeshing or mandatory GPU tessellation/compute is not needed for the first design.
5. **Ownership and bounded latency.** Keep the data-only model inside Paper, behind `SPaperModule`; use [the existing `SelfDrawn` dispatch](../src/WindowAnimations.tpp#L636-L672), `CShader`, and private depth-FBO precedent in [`PlanetGlRenderer::bind_output`](../src/workspace/planet_hop/PlanetGlRenderer.cpp#L428-L458), not a workspace-style import. One active animation owns its state through live and retained-snapshot drawing. Bounded shared CPU worker capacity; GL stays on the compositor thread. Publish generation-tagged consecutive fixed-step poses. Render interpolates; captures draw read-only and cannot consume RNG or advance time. No full-event recordings, render-thread waits, synchronous catch-up, thread per window or idle simulation. Bound initial construction as well as steady-state work; measure queue age. Pick simulation tier at event start using aspect, visible size and aggregate load; preserve existing contacts and quality of active events rather than switching topology on a frame spike. After stalls use the existing cancellation policy; on disable, destroy, monitor change or unload invalidate publications and release CPU/GPU resources in their owning contexts. Shared scheduling must not starve one open behind a burst of closes. Follow [style isolation](adr/0001-styles-are-independent.md), [core/renderer separation](adr/0002-compositor-independent-core.md) and [state ownership](adr/0003-window-animation-state-ownership.md).
6. **Configuration and replay.** Keep `window.options.paper` inheritance and per-direction overrides from [`settings.json`](../config/settings.json), including `duration` (open 0.6 s, close 0.5 s; 0.1–3 s). Keep `crumpliness` for loading intensity, `variation` for force/material spread, and `shading` for crease lighting rather than adding three synonymous keys. Make `crumple_size` control envelope size independently, `fold_density` the load/material correlation length rather than tessellation, and `paper_stiffness` elastic bend/yield, not texture contrast. `crumple_origin` locates the grip envelope; snapshot/clamp the pointer in window space for cursor origin, since `Place` does not yet carry a pointer. `force_direction` biases force orientation, `rotation_intensity` torque, `settling` damping. `unfold_tension` and `crease_persistence` govern opening only. `simulation_quality` limits solver/geometry cost independently of density. No user-facing solver iterations. One deliberate semantic change: existing `variation=0` promises identical takes; the new minimum is restrained but nonzero variation. Mix a session salt and independent event serial into separate RNG streams for forces, material and release, including on interruption. The debug replay input is the actual seed, geometry, config/tier and fixed-tick event history; do not make wall-clock thread scheduling part of material randomness. Paper movement remains undeformed.

## Evidence required after approval

First evaluate the failure most likely to invalidate this design: in continuous playback at native size, does dense, high-contrast content **disappear behind another fold while keeping its material distances**, then re-emerge on the same patch? Inspect the silhouette, edge crossings, crease directions and highlights, not a wireframe or only the endpoints. Test cold openings at short and default duration, close-during-open with different origin/forces, and exact native handoff. Record multiple seeds and deterministic replays; compare lengths/shear and layer order, not just screenshots. Check all requested aspect ratios, content types, density extremes, transparent/decorated windows, repetitions, cancellation, multiple concurrent windows, and nested workspace captures.

Measure model ticks, first pose, publication age, render-thread submission, delayed/disjoint-checked GPU queries and whole-frame presentation separately. Compare native, old Paper and new Paper under matched scenes. Record and time in separate runs. Exercise 60/120/144 Hz schedules offscreen/nested, run live tests in a private nested compositor, and distinguish VFR capture cadence from physical presentation. On today's connected modes, physical 120/144 Hz is unverified. Build/import checks, static analysis, headless mechanics and GPU pixel tests, unrelated style controls, resource teardown and continuous-motion visual review all remain **post-approval**. Nothing here establishes that a replacement already meets those gates.

## Implementation evidence

Measurements below are local, not promises for other GPUs or refresh rates. The original estimates above did not predict the cost of densely packed contact. The first adaptive solver took **203 ms** to prepare one opening; four sequential preparations took **1.37 s**. At 120 Hz, one dense opening consumed **7.70/18.56 ms median/p95** for `advance + frame`; the initial closing still occupied 72% of the original bounding-box area. These numbers disqualified that prototype.

In a four-seed CPU profile, narrowphase and closest-edge evaluation accounted for approximately three quarters of sampled cycles. Direct sweeps of unique vertex/face and edge/edge features, conservative relative-motion bounds and cached contact weights replaced duplicated triangle-pair work. On an isolated eight-layer, 200-node stress case, contact p95 fell from **64.18 to 10.34 ms** under compression, and **4.23 to 0.176 ms** while separated. Minimum separation under the compressed load was **98.4%** of the target thickness. The compressed figure is one artificial stress solve, *not* total animation cost per frame.

The first native candidate exposed two bugs that endpoint screenshots alone missed: a compact initial condition with crossing layers stalled opening, and a vertically inverted framebuffer made even a still-flat closing sheet display upside-down text. Captured row labels and material-space intersection measurements are part of the acceptance checks. A screenshot taken after removing the transformer cannot validate the orientation of the rendered sheet.

The shading path must preserve two independent facts: a folded surface changes *which part of the print is visible*, even at `shading=0`; changing `shading` changes only the light that reaches that visible surface. The directional shadow map includes folds hidden from the eye. Short-range layer shadowing, matte diffuse response and grazing thickness response use material geometry; they are not screen-space outlines. Contact-protected patches are not rounded through one another.

**Rendering check on actual print.** A 620×620 labeled-text capture was uploaded once and rendered through the production material with the same simulated positions, UVs, thickness and camera at `shading=0` and `1`. Across the 146-frame open/close sequence there were **zero alpha mismatches**. Fold shadows moved across the lettering on lower layers; unlit overlaps still hid the same regions of print. A separate nested capture resolved the initial orientation defect: the frame before native handoff differed from the upright print by **0.54/255 mean absolute RGB**, versus **47.14/255** if vertically flipped; the settled crop was pixel-identical.

**Cost of lighting, not of capture.** On the RTX 2070 SUPER at 1280×720, an intermediate solver's *physically crumpled* poses at full fold density and `shading=.7` cost **0.142 ms CPU submission and 0.195 ms GPU query elapsed per draw** for one window, **0.174 / 0.205 ms** per draw for four. 120 frames, fixed seeds, 30 input poses per window, warm buffers; the measurements include material reconstruction, VBO upload, depth and color draws and the compositor-oriented blit. They exclude model advancement, capturing the original window, the final host composite and vsync. GPU figures are elapsed time over a command stream with CPU submissions, not isolated shader execution or frame percentiles; disjoint flags were zero. The native fill/mesh and shadow attachments are reused across sequential windows on the same pass. Shadow depth storage is capped at 1024² independently of the eye buffer.

**Physical opening, not an authored initial pose.** A compact starting patch constructed from curved strips was fast, but a full-size recording looked like the same roll unspooling. That path was removed. Each fresh opening now starts from a flat sheet on a worker, crushes it with its own seeded distribution of external loads, and retains the resulting positions and signed plastic bends. No sequence of poses is saved. Opening then uses its own rim pulls, friction and local unloading before a final planar support brings the sheet to its exact material coordinates. Reversing a visible event keeps its material state. Hidden preparation is cancellable between physical steps, and is included in first-publication timings, not in GPU timings.

**Nested playback of the physical preparation.** In the four-window 1280×720 run `25f21de8`, four printed windows formed visibly different folded masses. Hashing individual, non-overlapping interior crops during the 1.2 s transitions gave **58–60 changed presentations/s opening**, **46–58/s closing**. On the slowest close, gap p95/max was **34/50 ms** despite regular whole-output timestamps. The near-flat opening crop before native handoff differed by **0.058/255** from the upright reference versus **46.87/255** vertically flipped; the settled image was exact. These are nested, recorded presentation intervals, not worker timings or proof of physical high-refresh scanout.

**Revisions driven by those recordings.** With the solver and seeds held constant, the worker was changed to publish a completed physical tick instead of waiting for an entire 60 Hz batch. No state is synthesized across changing topology and no constraint iterations are removed. For four dense, concurrent, default-duration closes at 120 Hz, the worst per-window p95 publication gap fell from **33.33 to 8.34 ms**; at 144 Hz, **34.72 to 13.89 ms**. Those are wall-paced calls to the real workers, not video frame timestamps. Under sustained overload the effect takes longer rather than skipping contact solves or waiting on the compositor thread. Replacing temporary trees and nested incidence lists in topology rebuilds with reusable, sorted arrays cut counted allocations in the 30-frame `paper.close` workload from **55,654 to 739**; this alone did not improve median solve time.

**Resolution and memory.** With the same physical pose bank and printed texture, one shaded draw averaged **0.394 ms at 2560×1440** and **0.747 ms at 3840×2160** over 200 frames on the RTX 2070 SUPER. Unshaded 4K was **0.242 ms**. All three `GL_EXT_disjoint_timer_query` intervals were non-disjoint. At 4K, assuming four bytes of storage per depth texel, the retained private color/depth pair (64-pixel growth increments) and 1024² light map require approximately **68 MiB per live or scratch renderer**, excluding the host work buffer. That renderer is shared by all windows on the same pass, not allocated four times for four windows. Separately, sampled glibc heap usage above process baseline for one model plus three output buffers peaked at **0.49–0.52 MiB**; four dense models used **1.83–2.41 MiB** (240 Hz samples, tcache disabled). This excludes worker stacks, graphics resources, and transient allocations freed between samples.

### Measured baseline: yielding grips, whole-sheet collision safeguard (`c06591be`)

[Individual printed sheet](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/square-printed-open-close-native.mp4) · [four simultaneous windows](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/dense-four-open-close-native.mp4) · [identical trajectory, unlit versus fully shaded](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/shading-comparison.mp4). The native clips retain their original size and timing; the comparison renders simulated poses offline at 60 samples/s. Its shaded and unlit halves had identical opacity at **every pixel in 146 frames**. Lighting changed the red channel by more than 8/255 on 8,951,151 of 35,253,561 covered pixel samples.

The full isolated Hyprland matrix recorded **6,142 frames in 102.7 s**. All 21 single-window settled captures matched their native controls exactly. Four simultaneous printed windows produced **58–60 changed presentations/s while opening, 59–60 while closing** in their separate interior crops; the longest gaps were 20 and 19 ms respectively. This excludes the first 100 ms and endpoint holds. A close during opening, 24 rapid map/unmap requests and three active-opening unload/reload cycles left no orphan clients. The recording contains **160 and 55 ms whole-output gaps during lifecycle stress**; total composite and recording cost cannot be attributed to one Paper draw.

Hashing every ordinary presentation, not just the default and dense cases, caught an exception: one 620² left-origin directional opening displayed **the same folded image for 433 ms** while frames continued arriving normally. It was not an endpoint hold. A deterministic native replay at seed 5120 reproduced a **550 ms hold**; the pure model also had a large error immediately before native handoff despite ending at the exact flat geometry. Endpoint equality and regular output timestamps alone would both miss this defect.

[Bridson, Fedkiw & Anderson, §7.4–7.5](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/cloth-sig02/cloth.pdf) explains why resolving one collision by backing up an entire cloth time step can freeze unrelated regions. Their separate geometric safeguard merges only colliding features into transient impact zones and tests again for secondary impacts. Even local rigidification can freeze a clump if applied as the primary solver. This is a useful distinction between maintaining layer order and actually letting layers release.

On the Ryzen 5 5600X, the [120 Hz paced test](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/shipping-cadence.csv) published four dense sheets at **8.34 ms p95** per window. A cold opening's first publication took **58–92 ms** with four active windows at that rate. At 144 Hz, the worst per-window closing p95 was **13.89 ms**, not one pose per refresh. Duration is simulation time: the nominal 500 ms closes took **508–625 ms** in the four-window 120 Hz run. At 60 Hz under contention they took as long as **785 ms**. The render thread does not synchronously catch up when the solver falls behind. At 720p the final shaded renderer's [GPU command-stream timers](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/gpu.log) averaged **0.157 ms per draw for one window, 0.150 ms per draw for four**. These are not end-to-end frame times.

**Remaining realism limits.** In a 20-seed [held-out contact audit](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/ccd-held-out.csv), there were no strict nonincident face piercings in 440 sampled poses. This test doesn't detect every coplanar overlap or prove that every possible seed is safe. Letting individual external grips yield when the paper is under strain reduced the worst measured local edge error from 246% to **93%**. Global area stayed above 94%, but that does not mean the print stays locally inextensible. Hard compression can still pinch lettering, and projecting a sharp fold can produce an overly triangular tip. Enabling extra cold refinement in a separate comparison added approximately 29 ms of preparation without consistently eliminating those spikes; it was not enabled. The effect is a physically driven layered sheet, **not a validated photorealistic material or a guaranteed 144 Hz animation**.

### Removing the whole-sheet stall

At seed 5120 the old solver repeatedly chose a zero time of impact for the entire sheet even though most of it had a valid proposed displacement. The replacement first removes only the closing normal component of residual impacts, using inverse-mass weights at the actual witnesses. It sweeps again for secondary impacts. Mutually blocking witnesses share a temporary, mass-weighted transport zone as a last resort; unrelated wings retain their motion. A regression exercises a resting triangle tip whose other two vertices would otherwise swing through a neighboring face. Separate fixtures check an unconstrained wing and conservation of free center-of-mass translation. The terminal check also runs when the frictional projection has already converged.

With **only** the collision code changed, the [matched seed-5120 capture](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/seed-5120-contact-only.mp4) went from 37 to 70 changed opening frames out of 72. Its longest changed-image gap was 20 ms instead of 550 ms and the final deformed-to-native RGB error was 0.0057/255 instead of 12.88/255. Keeping the [failing recording](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/seed-5120-before.mp4) distinguishes this fix from a different random draw, a faster recorder, or hiding the held image.

**A second failure was local, not a frozen sheet.** A tucked flap on seed 440 could still sit 174 pixels from flat one display sample before the native window appeared. A shared material edge cannot collide with itself. Two faces rotating about it could approach a zero-width wedge while another paper layer held them closed. The tested remedy applies a thickness- and face-altitude-dependent bend limit near a fully closed hinge. During opening, a signed counterbending moment acts around tight hinges without erasing their plastic memory. A bounded planar press still passes through swept contact checks. Exact native handoff requires both ends of the interpolation interval to be within half a capture pixel of rest, with a positive-definite symmetric part of each face's planar material Jacobian. This last test establishes a non-overlapping straightening path; elapsed time alone does not.

In the final build's [100-event independent model sweep](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/final-handoff-100.csv), four aspect ratios, seven origins, three force modes and two densities all finished at nominal model time. Maximum node error one 120 Hz sample before completion: **0.601 px**. In [short-duration and previously jammed cases](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/radius-certified-extremes.csv), the 100 ms opens had 1.41–3.89 px left **8.33 ms before** completion; this is not the error immediately before the subpixel handoff. A [separate geometry audit](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/final-feature-crossings.csv) found no strict interior edge/face piercings in 770 sampled poses, including opposite edges of neighboring face fans. The minimum total material area was 94.78% of rest. At the worst sample one short edge still had 112.5% length error; zero observed piercings does not imply inextensibility or rule out coplanar overlap.

The [final unlit/shaded comparison](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/crease-adaptive/final-lighting.mp4) uses identical folded geometry, UVs, camera and print. Across 146 frames, changing `shading` from zero to one changed no alpha values; lighting changed the red channel by more than 8/255 on 12,462,288 of 39,369,814 covered samples. Matte crease highlights and layer shadows travel with the folds; corners under severe compression can still look too pointed.

### Why an exact endpoint still sprang

On opening, `073f7b20` moved every node toward its original material position after the bend solver. The fraction of the remaining distance removed per tick increased toward one as time ran out. The solver then measured velocity **after** this correction, so the next prediction carried the imposed displacement forward as momentum. A correct native screenshot proved the destination, not the path. The worker did not switch to a rectangular window at 75%.

Two isolated controls separated the causes. At 760×540, 3 s, seed 440, the final 35% contained eight renewed peaks in RMS surface speed. Removing only the imposed displacement from the outgoing velocity eliminated cumulative increases in RMS distance from rest on this trajectory. Removing the whole positional pull instead left **61.45 px** maximum position error at nominal completion. The same experiment on a 620² directional opening (1.2 s, seed 5120) left **50.71 px**. Simply reducing the spring or increasing damping did not supply the missing unfolding mechanism.

The replacement keeps the signed plastic hinges, adaptive topology, swept contact and matte renderer. Seeded grips exert **in-plane traction at the rim**, not position targets across the printed interior. The immutable edge lengths transmit that tension. Opposed pressing surfaces approach with decreasing clearance; a point inside the gap feels no normal pressure. Contacting folds yield less to the press. Large bends counter-rotate first; a crease that is held under pressure can relax even after its angular motion has nearly stopped. The tools come to rest before the deadline so the last wrinkles can quiet under a steady load.

Opening uses an overdamped solve: measured node velocities still transfer through refinement and reversal, but hand or platen displacement is not injected as a free-flight impulse on the next tick. Exact native geometry is accepted only after two consecutive poses have an injective projection and every node is within 0.25 capture pixels of rest. Maximum material speed is 6 px/s, residual hinge angle 0.01 rad, and angular speed 0.1 rad/s. If not quiet, the worker continues the same physical solve; it cannot fade out or substitute a rectangle for a folded sheet.

**Geometry proof.** At 1280×720, seed 440, the 3 s opening's surface height range at 70%, 80%, 90% and 95% was **6.34, 1.86, 0.045 and 0.0012 px**. A rigid tilt or translation cannot pass the separate normal-spread regression. In the [35 independent openings](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/unfolding/after-events.csv), the median cumulative movement *away* from the native position of the worst of 25 material probes in the last 35% fell from [26.07 px](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/unfolding/before-events.csv) to **0 px**. One corner on a 2100×690 directional sheet moved 12.8 px farther from rest between 65% and 68% before unfolding monotonically through the final quarter; real fold release need not be strictly radial. Maximum sampled material displacement across the last 120 Hz interval was 0.119 px.

All 35 reached the exact native pose at the configured model duration. So did [20 additional stress events](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/unfolding/stress-model.csv), including 100 ms minimum-tension openings and 2600×240 strips. Grips can travel farther along the long material axis without increasing the through-sheet step. A 100 ms opening uses 64 steps (96 on long strips) and allows at least 25 ms of settling under stationary tools. [21 fresh closing controls](../.project-manager/evidence/paper/unfolding/closing-control.csv) matched the previous solver bit-for-bit in sampled positions, UVs, triangle indices and crease weights. These are model checks, not claims about a loaded compositor's wall clock.

### Reproducing the checks

```sh
cmake -S . -B build -DBUILD_TESTING=ON
cmake --build build
ctest --test-dir build --output-on-failure
EGL_PLATFORM=surfaceless build/paper-material-tests --crumpled 240 4 .7 1280 720
cmake -S tools/dev -B build/dev/paper -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release
cmake --build build/dev/paper --target paper-cadence
build/dev/paper/bench/paper-cadence
python tests/nested/paper_evidence.py --label replay-5120 --directional-left --replay-seed 5120 --settle 1.8
python tests/nested/paper_evidence.py --label release-440 --unfolding --replay-seed 440 --settle 3.6
python tests/nested/paper_unfolding.py build/paper-evidence/release-440
```

`PaperCrumple::begin` accepts an explicit seed for repeatable force loading. `paper-cadence` reports first publication, completed wall time and maximum gap between genuinely distinct published buffers at 60/120/144 Hz; it does **not** claim physical display presentation at those rates. The `--crumpled` harness generates its seeded pose bank before starting the GPU query. `tests/nested/paper_evidence.py --help` describes the separate real compositor test. Its capture and client are private; there is no reason to load an experimental build into the desktop session to run any of these checks.
